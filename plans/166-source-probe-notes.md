## 5. Implications for the S1 conflict policy

Hand-written verdict, 2026-07-21, from sections 1-4 above:

- **Dedupe before any conflict logic.** 3788 of 7761 raw events (49%) are the SAME record replicated across roots (claude-data is a sync of live; probe-fixture is a frozen copy of claude-data). Without (sessionId, recordUuid) dedupe, nearly half the "cross-source evidence" is phantom agreement. This must be the merge pipeline's first stage.
- **Replicas diverge by prefix growth, not by contradiction.** All 4 diverged JSONL copies (section 2) are one copy strictly LONGER than the other (sync lag: live RevEng logs grew after the claude-data sync; claude-data's jot log grew after the probe-fixture copy). Per-record dedupe absorbs this automatically — no "which copy wins" policy is needed at file level.
- **Root-relative path agreement ALONE is not an identity rule.** The biggest genuine-overlap cluster is `jot` vs `jot-backup` — sibling REPO COPIES sharing rel-paths (e.g. `audit/build_call_graph.py`, 37 events across both). Conflict candidates 1-4 are these siblings receiving different edits hours apart: merging them into one timeline by rel-path would interleave two diverging repos. The task-137 rule (rel-path agreement + CONTENT agreement) is confirmed as necessary: content must match at the merge point before two roots' timelines are joined.
- **Cross-cwd edits to one absolute path are real and common.** `scripts/jot_plugin_orchestrator.py` (abs path under `~/Programming/jot`) was edited by sessions from FOUR project folders (jot, jot-backup, claude-code-src, RevEng). For a single absolute path, timestamp interleave across sources is the right composition — this validates the planned policy for the same-file case.
- **No true same-instant contradictions found.** All 5 conflict candidates are explicable: either sibling-repo divergence (jot vs jot-backup — an identity question, not a conflict question) or alternating legitimate edit streams from different cwds. Adjacent-edit content-hash inequality is NOT a conflict signal (different sequential edits always differ); a real conflict detector must check lineage (does the later edit's old_string match the state the earlier source left behind), which is what the engine's stale-edit-base machinery already does per-source.
- **Verdict: the planned policy survives, amended.** "Interleave strictly by timestamp + health-note conflicts" is validated for events targeting the same file identity, but must run AFTER (1) record-level dedupe and (2) the content-gated identity rule that keeps diverged sibling repos (jot vs jot-backup) apart. Health-note conflicts should fire on lineage mismatch, not on mere content-hash inequality.
- **File-history evidence is concentrated in the backups, not live.** Only 10 jot-editing sessions still have live `~/.claude/file-history` dirs vs 139 in claude-data (129 of which are replicated into probe-fixture). The multi-source BackupReader must accept several file-history roots and dedupe session dirs by UUID across them (same-session dirs are copies).
- **Parse tolerance was not stressed at the line level** (0 unparseable lines) — but the strict record-TYPE allow-set still matters (s87/s32 lessons); the probe bypassed record typing entirely.
